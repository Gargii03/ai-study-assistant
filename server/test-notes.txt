Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration

Photosynthesis is the biochemical process by which photoautotrophs (like green plants, algae, and cyanobacteria) convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.
The general chemical equation for photosynthesis is:
6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2

Key Stages of Photosynthesis:
1. Light-Dependent Reactions: Occur in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs photons, exciting electrons. Water is split (photolysis) to release O2 as a byproduct, producing ATP and NADPH.
2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions): Occurs in the stroma. Carbon fixation catalyzed by the enzyme RuBisCO uses ATP and NADPH to convert carbon dioxide into 3-carbon sugars (G3P), which eventually form glucose.

Cellular Respiration is the metabolic process by which cells break down glucose to yield ATP energy for cellular work.
The general chemical equation is:
C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + ~36-38 ATP

Key Stages of Cellular Respiration:
1. Glycolysis: Occurs in the cytoplasm. Breaks 1 glucose into 2 pyruvate molecules, producing a net 2 ATP and 2 NADH. Does not require oxygen (anaerobic).
2. Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle): Takes place in the mitochondrial matrix. Generates electron carriers: 6 NADH, 2 FADH2, and 2 ATP per glucose molecule.
3. Oxidative Phosphorylation & Electron Transport Chain (ETC): Occurs on the inner mitochondrial cristae membrane. High-energy electrons flow through complex I-IV, creating a proton gradient across the inner membrane. ATP synthase uses this chemiosmotic gradient to generate approximately 30-34 ATP. Oxygen is the terminal electron acceptor, forming H2O.
